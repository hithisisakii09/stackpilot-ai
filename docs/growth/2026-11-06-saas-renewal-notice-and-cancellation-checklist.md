## 1. Why this topic is distinct

StackPilot already has a total-cost-of-ownership guide, a usage-meter buyer guide, CRM exit-evidence research and a seat-billing/unused-license audit draft. This brief does not repeat their cost arithmetic, usage units, CRM export inventory or user-versus-seat explanations.

It addresses a different decision:

**What must a buyer verify before a renewal or cancellation deadline so that the contract, billing, service access and data handoff do not surprise the team?**

A billing-page renewal date is not necessarily the contract's cancellation-notice deadline. Turning off recurring billing may be different from cancelling immediately. The purchase channel may determine where cancellation must be submitted. The date a service stops, the date data becomes unavailable and the date data is permanently deleted can be separate events.

The useful StackPilot contribution is a small-team, vendor-neutral checklist and renewal register that a buyer can complete from their own order form, invoice and administrator console. It is not a contract repository, renewal-alert system or vendor-account integration.

### Microsoft 365 for business — cancellation windows and data lifecycle

**Primary source:** [What happens to my data and access when my Microsoft 365 for business subscription ends?](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365/commerce/subscriptions/what-if-my-subscription-expires)  
**Visible source update:** 2026-06-08. **Checked:** 2026-11-06.

The page says it applies to Microsoft 365 for business subscriptions, including Enterprise and Volume Licensing. It warns that some subscriptions can only be cancelled during a limited window after purchase or renewal; after that window, the buyer may need to turn off recurring billing so the subscription ends at its term date. It describes Active → Expired → Disabled → Deleted lifecycle states, while noting that purchase channel/program changes the time spent in each state.

For most offers in most countries/regions, the page lists 30 days in Expired and 90 days in Disabled. It says customer data left behind might be deleted after 90 days and no later than 180 days after cancellation. During Disabled, admins can access and back up data while ordinary users cannot access Microsoft 365 services. The page also says that explicitly deleting a subscription skips the Expired and Disabled stages and immediately deletes SharePoint Online data and content, including OneDrive.

**Critical qualifications:** the Expired state no longer applies from 2026-02-09 to license-based subscriptions purchased directly through a Microsoft Customer Agreement (MCA). Enterprise, Volume Licensing, Open Value and CSP lifecycles can differ; the page directs CSP customers to Partner Center and says exact timing depends on the specific agreement. These durations must not be presented as universal or as an India-specific guarantee.

### Atlassian Cloud — billing experience, product and Marketplace distinctions

**Primary sources:** [Deactivate a subscription](https://support.atlassian.com/subscriptions-and-billing/docs/cancel-a-subscription/) and [Track storage and move data across apps](https://support.atlassian.com/organization-administration/docs/track-storage-and-move-data-across-products/).  
**Visible update date:** not displayed in the retrieved pages. **Checked:** 2026-11-06.

Atlassian says it is rolling out an improved billing experience, so an account may use either the original or improved flow. In the improved experience, deactivation is scheduled for 15 days after the current subscription period ends. The cancellation page says site access continues for 15 days after the subscription period ends, then the site is deactivated; product-data retention varies by plan. Its separate storage/data page lists 15 days for trials and 60 days for Free, Standard, Premium or Enterprise plans for the covered Jira/Confluence app data. Do not assume the table covers every Atlassian product, billing system or Marketplace app.

The cancellation page says Marketplace-app data-retention and reactivation periods are controlled by the Marketplace app vendor, not Atlassian. It also says the original billing experience generally terminates most plans at the end of the current billing cycle, with no credit for cancelling before the cycle ends; Enterprise cancellation is different. Free-plan app deactivation and trial treatment also differ.

**Critical qualifications:** identify the billing experience, app/product, plan, collection, Marketplace dependencies and whether the customer is on Enterprise before quoting a timeline. Cancelling a subscription, deleting an individual app and removing a Marketplace app are not interchangeable actions.

### Zoom — purchase channel, add-ons and cloud recordings

**Primary source:** [Canceling your Zoom subscription](https://support.zoom.com/hc/en/article?id=zm_kb&sysparm_article=KB0066687).  
**Visible update date:** not displayed in the retrieved page. **Checked:** 2026-11-06.

Zoom says customers who purchased directly from Zoom can usually cancel a subscription or add-on through the web portal before the next renewal date. Cancellation stops future renewals; paid service remains available until the end of the current plan term. The cancellation becomes effective on the next renewal date. Active add-ons must be cancelled separately before the base plan, and cancellation does not automatically generate a refund.

The page limits self-service cancellation to eligible direct purchases and an account owner, admin or role with billing privileges. A direct sales quote may require an account executive or support; App Store/Google Play purchases must be cancelled through the relevant store; an organization-managed account may not allow the individual user to cancel. Zoom says cloud recordings remain available for 30 days after a paid subscription ends, after which they are permanently deleted.

**Critical qualifications:** this is not a universal Zoom cancellation promise for resellers, app-store purchases, quote-based agreements or organization-managed accounts. The 30-day statement is about cloud recordings, not every Zoom data class.

## 5. Unpublished article draft

# SaaS Renewal Checklist: Find the Notice Deadline Before You Auto-Renew

A software renewal can be missed even when a team knows the subscription's end date. The date shown in a billing dashboard may not be the date by which a customer must send a non-renewal notice. A cancellation request can also have a different effect from turning off recurring billing, removing an add-on or deleting an account.

Before you renew, downgrade or leave a SaaS product, put the agreement, billing record and data-exit plan in one place. The goal is not to cancel everything. It is to make the decision before the contract makes it for you.

This checklist is general buyer education. Your signed order form, amendments, purchase channel, applicable law and vendor confirmation control the actual outcome.

## 1. Record the dates separately

Do not keep one field called “renewal date.” Record each event separately:

| Date or deadline | What it means | Evidence to save |
|---|---|---|
| Contract start date | When the current commitment began | Signed order form or agreement |
| Contract end date | When the current term is scheduled to end | Order form and amendments |
| Renewal date | When a new term or billing period may begin | Agreement and billing portal |
| Non-renewal notice deadline | Last date to deliver notice under the agreement | Exact clause and written confirmation |
| Next billing date | When the next invoice or payment is expected | Invoice and billing portal |
| Cancellation-effective date | When cancellation or non-renewal takes effect | Confirmation email or case ID |
| Access cutoff date | When users or administrators lose service access | Vendor lifecycle documentation |
| Data-retention/deletion date | When recoverable data may be permanently removed | Product-specific retention policy |

The notice deadline can precede the contract end date. The access cutoff can follow the end date. The data-deletion date can follow the access cutoff. Never infer one date from another.

## 2. Find the controlling agreement and purchase channel

Collect the current order form, master service agreement, renewal notice, amendments, invoice and any reseller or marketplace terms. Record:

- Contracting legal entity and billing account.
- Product, edition, SKU, add-ons and committed quantities.
- Whether the subscription was purchased directly, through a reseller/CSP, through an app store, through a marketplace or under an enterprise quote.
- Contract term, renewal term and any minimum commitment.
- Auto-renewal language and the exact notice period.
- Required notice method, recipient, address or portal.
- Any price-change, usage true-up, early-termination, refund or closeout clauses.
- Which party is authorized to submit notice.

A support article can explain the normal self-service path, but it does not replace the signed contract. If the invoice, portal and agreement show different dates or entities, stop and ask the vendor or reseller for written clarification before acting.

## 3. Check how renewal and cancellation actually work

Look for the difference between:

1. **Turn off recurring billing:** the current term may continue to its scheduled end.
2. **Submit non-renewal notice:** the agreement may require a specific notice period and delivery method.
3. **Cancel immediately:** this may be available only in a defined window or under specific terms.
4. **Remove an add-on:** this may be a separate action from cancelling the base subscription.
5. **Delete an account or product:** this can have data consequences that are different from ending future renewals.

The vendor examples below show why a single “cancel any time” rule is unsafe:

- Microsoft 365 for business documents limited cancellation windows for some subscriptions. If the window has passed, turning off recurring billing can let the subscription end at its term date. The lifecycle depends on the purchase program and channel.
- Atlassian documents both original and improved billing experiences. The steps, timing and data handling differ; Enterprise and Marketplace apps can have separate conditions.
- Zoom's self-service flow is for eligible direct purchases. App-store purchases, quote-based subscriptions and organization-managed accounts can require a different route. Add-ons are cancelled separately.

Use these examples to identify questions, not to assume your own contract follows the same rule.

## 4. Build a data-exit plan before sending notice

Make a product-by-product export inventory. Confirm:

- Which administrator can export the data.
- Whether the export includes records, relationships, attachments, files, comments, activity history, audit logs and configuration.
- Whether data is held by a marketplace app or another processor.
- Which formats are available and whether they can be imported elsewhere.
- Whether download links expire.
- Whether a backup can be restored and who will test it.
- Whether the vendor offers a post-cancellation recovery period.
- Which data classes have separate retention or deletion rules.
- Whether the contract, DPA or retention policy requires a particular deletion request.

Do not treat a vendor's backup as your own migration copy. Do not assume that a reactivated subscription restores data after permanent deletion.

### Three official examples worth checking

**Microsoft 365 for business:** Microsoft's lifecycle documentation says customer data can become admin-only during the Disabled state and may be deleted after 90 days, no later than 180 days after cancellation, for the described lifecycle. It separately warns that explicitly deleting a subscription can immediately delete SharePoint Online and OneDrive content. Purchase program and agreement terms affect timing.

**Atlassian Cloud:** the documentation distinguishes subscription deactivation from app deletion and describes plan-dependent retention. Marketplace-app retention is set by the app vendor. Some individual product-removal paths can delete data immediately, so identify the exact app and action before proceeding.

**Zoom:** the support page says cloud recordings remain available for 30 days after a paid subscription ends, then are permanently deleted. This statement is about cloud recordings; it should not be generalized to every Zoom data class.

Export critical data while the subscription and authorized administrator access are still available. If the vendor offers a recovery window, treat it as a contingency—not the primary backup plan.

## 5. Review the money that can remain after a decision

Before approval, ask the billing owner to reconcile:

- Remaining annual or multi-year commitment.
- Renewal term and minimum quantities.
- Seat or license commitments (covered separately in StackPilot's seat-billing guide).
- Usage overages, true-ups and metered add-ons (covered separately in the usage-meter guide).
- Taxes, outstanding invoices and final-period charges.
- Proration, credits and refund eligibility.
- Reseller or marketplace billing that may continue separately.
- Any service that remains active because it is attached to a bundle or collection.

Do not calculate expected savings from list prices or assumed refunds. Use the actual agreement, invoice and vendor-confirmed quantities.

## 6. Use a reminder schedule that follows the contract

A reminder calendar is an internal control, not a substitute for the contract's notice deadline. A small team might choose review checkpoints at 120, 90, 60 and 30 days before renewal, but the schedule must be moved earlier if the agreement requires more notice.

At each checkpoint:

- **Early review:** confirm the owner, agreement, renewal date and notice deadline.
- **Usage/value review:** ask the business owner whether the product is still required and whether the current quantity is justified.
- **Options review:** compare renewal, downgrade, replacement and exit requirements without assuming a price or feature.
- **Decision review:** obtain budget and service-owner approval before sending a binding notice.
- **Closeout:** retain the submitted notice, vendor acknowledgement, final invoice and export/restore evidence.

If the notice deadline is already near or unclear, escalate to the contract owner or vendor immediately. Do not rely on a calendar reminder alone.

## 7. Copyable SaaS renewal register

| Field | Example of what to record |
|---|---|
| Vendor and product | Exact product and edition |
| Contracting entity | Legal entity on the order form |
| Purchase channel | Direct, reseller, CSP, app store, marketplace or enterprise quote |
| Internal owner | Named accountable team/person |
| Billing administrator | Role authorized to view or change billing |
| Agreement reference | Contract/order form ID (store securely in your own system) |
| Contract start/end | Dates from the current signed term |
| Renewal date | Date shown in the agreement/portal |
| Notice deadline | Exact date and clause |
| Notice method/recipient | Portal, email, address or named contact from contract |
| Auto-renew status | Verified from the current agreement/portal |
| Renewal term | Duration and minimum commitment |
| Add-ons/dependencies | Separate subscriptions or marketplace apps |
| Usage/seat commitments | Source-backed quantities from invoice/contract |
| Data export owner | Authorized administrator |
| Export and restore test | PASS / FAIL / NOT TESTED |
| Retention/deletion policy | Product-specific source and date checked |
| Decision | Renew / renegotiate / downgrade / exit / hold |
| Approval and evidence | Decision owner, date and confirmation reference |

Do not put passwords, API keys, payment-card details, customer records or confidential contracts into a public worksheet. Keep contract IDs and supporting documents in the organization's approved storage.

## 8. Questions to send the vendor or reseller

1. What exact date and time zone is the non-renewal notice deadline for this agreement?
2. Which entity and channel must receive notice, and what form of acknowledgement proves receipt?
3. Does turning off recurring billing stop only the next renewal, or does it change the current term?
4. Are any add-ons, marketplace apps, usage packs or bundled products cancelled separately?
5. What committed fees, taxes, true-ups or outstanding charges remain after notice?
6. Which administrator can export each data class, and what is excluded from the export?
7. When does user access end, when does administrator access end, and when is each data class permanently deleted?
8. Is there a recovery period, and what action is required to restore service?
9. Does the answer differ by plan, purchase region, reseller, enterprise agreement or billing system?
10. Can the vendor confirm the answers in writing against the contract/order form reference?

Save the response with the agreement. If an answer conflicts with the contract, request a written amendment or clarification rather than relying on a support-chat summary.

## 9. How StackPilot fits — and what it does not do

StackPilot's current software-cost calculator and requirements/checklist tools are buyer-entered, browser-only decision aids. They can help a reader organize values and questions they choose to enter. They do not:

- Connect to a vendor billing account, inbox, contract repository or HR system.
- Read a contract or determine a legally binding notice deadline.
- Verify current vendor prices, plan entitlements or regional eligibility.
- Send renewal reminders, cancellation notices or vendor messages.
- Cancel, downgrade or renew a subscription.
- Back up, export, retain or delete customer data.
- Calculate verified savings, commissions or conversion outcomes.

Use the guide as a prompt for your own contract and administrator review, not as a substitute for vendor confirmation or legal advice.
