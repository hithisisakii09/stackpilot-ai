## 1. Why this topic is distinct

Existing StackPilot research already covers:
- SaaS total cost of ownership and user-entered cost calculations;
- usage meters, quota units and overage caveats;
- user-versus-seat billing and unused-license audits;
- renewal notice, cancellation, data-access and deletion windows; and
- CRM export and migration evidence.

This brief addresses a different buying decision:

**When a SaaS vendor says “99.9% uptime” or “support SLA,” what is actually covered, how is failure measured, and what must a customer do to qualify for a remedy?**

A percentage alone is not a complete service-level commitment. The covered product, plan, measurement period, definition of downtime, exclusions, evidence source, claim deadline, remedy and purchase channel matter. A public status page, an uptime monitor, a support-response target and a contractual availability SLA are different things.

The useful StackPilot contribution is a neutral, buyer-entered SLA evidence checklist that helps a small team compare the terms it has actually received. It is not an uptime monitor, contract repository, legal-review service, incident-alert system or automated SLA calculator.

### Important distinctions from the evidence

1. **The same percentage can have different practical meaning.** HubSpot's definition is a critical full outage with no workaround; Atlassian's definition uses request error rate for covered experiences; Kinsta uses downtime of live applications as calculated by its monitoring. These are not directly comparable without reading each definition.
2. **A credit is not necessarily a refund.** The examples above describe future invoice credits or other non-cash remedies, and some identify the credit as the exclusive remedy.
3. **Claim windows differ.** The examples above describe 15 days after the month for Atlassian, 20 days after the relevant month for HubSpot and 30 days after downtime for Kinsta. Never reuse one vendor's deadline for another.
4. **A support response target is not a resolution target.** HubSpot's terms say it attempts to respond within one business day for email/in-app support but do not promise a specific response time.
5. **A migration SLA is not an uptime SLA.** Cloudways' 24-hour-per-site migration timeframe describes a service request, not monthly service availability.
6. **Status pages are not contracts.** A status page or monitoring screenshot may help document an incident, but the customer's contract controls the definition, measurement, exclusions and remedy.

### Simple uptime arithmetic (illustrative only)

A 30-day month contains 43,200 minutes. If the entire month were the measurement window and no exclusions applied:

| Nominal availability | Arithmetic downtime allowance |
|---|---:|
| 99.9% | 43.2 minutes |
| 99.95% | 21.6 minutes |
| 99.99% | 4.32 minutes |

This is arithmetic, not a vendor-specific SLA calculation. Actual contracts may use a calendar month or billing period, exclude maintenance or other events, define downtime narrowly, use per-request thresholds, and set minimum outage thresholds before credits are available.

## 5. Unpublished article draft

# SaaS SLA Checklist: Uptime, Exclusions, Service Credits and Claim Deadlines

A software vendor's uptime percentage is only the beginning of an SLA review. Before choosing a plan, find out which service is covered, how downtime is counted, what is excluded and how quickly a customer must submit a claim.

A 99.9% headline can be less useful than a lower percentage with a clear measurement method and a remedy your team can actually use. A support team that aims to reply within one business day is not necessarily promising to resolve the incident in one day. A migration service that targets completion within 24 hours is not promising 99.9% monthly availability.

Use this checklist to compare the actual service terms supplied to your business—not marketing summaries alone.

## 1. Confirm the exact product and plan

Start with the documents that govern your purchase:

- Order form, quote or subscription schedule.
- Master agreement and product-specific terms.
- Service Level Agreement and any support schedule.
- Jurisdiction-specific terms and reseller agreement, if applicable.
- Product edition, add-ons, covered components and region.

Record the legal vendor entity, product name, plan, account region, purchase channel and document version. A plan name by itself may not establish that every product or feature is eligible for the SLA.

## 2. Read the downtime definition before comparing percentages

Ask what counts as downtime:

- A complete outage, or a degraded feature?
- Does one affected customer count, or must a percentage of requests fail?
- Are API, admin, authentication and reporting endpoints covered?
- Does a workaround mean the event is not counted?
- Is the measurement based on the vendor's own monitoring or customer evidence?
- Is the period a calendar month, UTC month or customer billing period?

For example, the HubSpot terms dated February 2, 2026 define downtime as a critical full outage with no workaround across a significant portion of production. Atlassian's April 30, 2026 SLA uses request-error definitions for covered experiences. Kinsta's SLA calculates downtime for live applications using its own monitoring. Those definitions should not be treated as equivalent.

## 3. List the exclusions and maintenance windows

Copy the exclusions from the applicable contract. Common categories in the reviewed examples include:

- Scheduled or emergency maintenance.
- Internet, telecommunications or customer network problems.
- Customer equipment, data, configuration or misuse.
- Third-party products and integrations.
- Force majeure and events outside the provider's control.
- Free, beta, sandbox or otherwise ineligible services.

Record the maintenance time zone and whether planned maintenance is included in the measurement. Kinsta's published documentation, for example, describes a daily maintenance period based on the data center's local time. Atlassian's SLA excludes routine scheduled and reasonable emergency maintenance. HubSpot's terms exclude specified maintenance and other causes.

Do not assume that every short interruption will count toward a service-credit claim.

## 4. Separate uptime, support and service-request commitments

Create a separate row for each type of commitment:

| Commitment type | What it answers | What it does not automatically promise |
|---|---|---|
| Availability / uptime SLA | How eligible service availability is measured | Resolution of every incident or compensation for every outage |
| Support response target | When support aims or commits to acknowledge a request | When the root cause will be fixed |
| Resolution target | When a defined issue should be resolved, if one is actually promised | A guaranteed fix when dependencies or customer action are involved |
| Migration / service-request SLA | Expected completion time for a defined request | Monthly platform uptime |

Cloudways' published 24-hour-per-site migration SLA is an example of a migration-service timeframe, not an uptime percentage. HubSpot's one-business-day support response language is an attempt to respond, not a guaranteed resolution time.

## 5. Check the service-credit process

A service credit may be limited by a threshold, formula, cap and claim deadline. Record each separately:

- Minimum downtime or number of failed measurement periods.
- Whether consecutive months are required.
- Who determines the outage and what evidence is accepted.
- How the claim is submitted and which account role can submit it.
- Deadline and time zone.
- Whether the credit applies to the affected product only.
- Whether the credit is cash, a refund or only a future invoice adjustment.
- Whether credits expire or are capped.
- Whether a reseller receives the credit first.
- Whether service credits are the exclusive contractual remedy.

The reviewed vendor examples demonstrate why this matters: the claim window is not universal, and a high uptime number does not guarantee a cash refund.

## 6. Keep incident evidence before the claim window closes

If an incident affects your business, record:

1. Start and end times with time zone.
2. Affected product, tenant, feature and user group.
3. Error messages, request IDs and relevant screenshots.
4. Vendor status-page incident URL and update timestamps.
5. Your own monitoring output, if permitted by your policies.
6. Support ticket number, severity and vendor responses.
7. The contract section and claim deadline.
8. Any workaround or customer-side dependency that may affect eligibility.

A public status page or third-party monitor can support an internal timeline, but it may not be the contractual source of truth. Atlassian's current SLA, for example, identifies Atlassian's monitoring and logging as the source of truth for its calculation. Follow the actual agreement's evidence process.

## 8. Questions to send the vendor before signing

- Which exact product features and endpoints are included in the SLA?
- Does the same commitment apply to our plan, region and purchase channel?
- How is partial degradation measured?
- What maintenance and third-party exclusions apply?
- Which system is the source of truth for uptime?
- What is the claim deadline, and who can submit the request?
- Are credits refundable or applied only to a future invoice?
- Does a reseller receive the credit before the customer?
- What are the support response and resolution commitments separately?
- What backup, recovery and data-export commitments are in the applicable agreement?

Ask for the answers in the order form or signed service schedule where they are commercially important. Keep the written response with the version of the terms that applies to your subscription.

## 9. Use a neutral buyer checklist—not a vendor score based on one number

A useful comparison should preserve unknowns and exceptions. Do not rank vendors solely by 99.9%, 99.95% or 99.99%. Compare the covered product, measurement definition, exclusions, claim process, support scope and remedy that actually apply to your purchase.

StackPilot's requirements checklist can help you record buyer-entered questions and evidence status. It does not verify a vendor contract, test uptime, monitor incidents or decide whether a customer qualifies for service credits. Confirm important terms directly with the vendor and obtain appropriate legal review.
